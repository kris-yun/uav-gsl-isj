# External-neighbor boundary: PHOENIX-UNet and Zebra

Date: 2026-09-23  
Purpose: freeze the novelty boundary before any M6 G1 training.

## PHOENIX-UNet — direct near neighbor, not a foundation-model collision

Official repository HEAD audited: `9c4c418aeab53a9fc14d1bc73a9afd652f187e9b`.

Verified from the official code and README:

- `img` has three channels: building mask, Gaussian plume prior, and source
  map. `meta` is the concatenation of source and meteorology vectors.
- The meteorology vector is global wind speed/direction/stability; no
  obstacle-resolved local wind tensor is consumed.
- The U-Net is instantiated and optimized from scratch. No pretrained
  backbone or cross-task checkpoint transfer is present.
- The released dataset is one fixed Chemical Park geometry generated by
  GRAMM/GRAL, with steady-state concentration fields over discrete
  meteorology/source settings (about 4,320 source–meteorology cases; the
  archive is about 14.7 GB).
- The canonical split is source-ID disjoint, but the train/validation/test
  lists share all meteorology values. It therefore does not establish
  wind-disjoint or cross-geometry transfer.

PHOENIX directly covers a task-specific source-conditioned plume surrogate:
`geometry + Gaussian prior + source + global meteorology -> concentration`.
Therefore M6 must not claim the first physics-enhanced gas surrogate, the
first source/wind-conditioned plume predictor, unseen-source/wind prediction,
or surrogate speedup as its novelty. A second direct prior-art boundary is
physics-guided source-conditioned neural forward modeling for GSL itself
(Ruiz et al., [arXiv:2405.04151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04151)); M6 cannot
claim the first neural source-conditioned GSL surrogate or the first neural
forward model used for inverse localization.

Two further claims are also unavailable: hazardous-chemical dispersion
transfer learning already exists (Han et al.,
[PSEP 188 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psep.2024.05.125)), and a learned
CFD surrogate embedded in Bayesian/particle-filter gas-emission inversion has
been reported (Newman et al., [arXiv:2506.14597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14597)).
Thus neither “gas-dispersion transfer learning” nor “learned forward model
inside a Bayesian source posterior” is sufficient M6 novelty by itself.

The remaining M6 delta is narrower:

> Transfer a released dynamics-lifted geometry foundation representation into
> an indoor GADEN candidate-source forward model, inject each candidate source
> after the frozen environment representation, and test whether this lowers
> plume-data requirements while improving truth-containing source rank across
> Houses against matched scratch, PHOENIX, and learned-forward-model controls.
> The external pretrained transfer must be the measured cause of the gain;
> PMFS posterior/planner integration is the fixed evaluation endpoint, not the
> novelty claim by itself.

Official PHOENIX sources: [repository](https://github.com/Jyyd/PHOENIX-UNet),
[paper DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.buildenv.2026.114797), and
[Zenodo dataset](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17008799).

The PHOENIX public pipeline is also a static concentration-field surrogate:
although the archive contains time-series material, the released preprocessing
and Dataset path train on the steady `con_data_98` field and do not expose a
temporal rollout/world-model interface.

## Zebra — promising external paradigm, not a ready M6 replacement

The verified ICML 2025 paper is
[Zebra](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/serrano25a.html), with
[official code](https://github.com/LouisSerrano/zebra). It tokenizes physical
fields with a VQ-VAE and trains a LLaMA-style autoregressive model. Its
context consists of several trajectories from one PDE environment; a query
initial condition then asks for a new forward trajectory. The published
tasks are PDE forward systems (Burgers, heat, wave, vorticity, Gray-Scott,
etc.), not gas-source inversion or PMFS source ranking.

The official repository requires the tokenizer and LLM training pipeline and
does not provide a verified ready-to-use ICML checkpoint for immediate PMFS
inference. Mapping it to PMFS would require a new demonstration format,
source/wind/geometry tokenization, and a field-bank training contract.

Decision: keep Zebra as a monitored M7 backup; do not split current resources
from M6 until a public checkpoint and a tiny source-query sandbox are both
verified. M6 remains the primary feasibility line, with the PHOENIX boundary
and source-rank/low-data tests frozen above.
