\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

\subsubsection{Environment and Observation Model}

Let $\mathcal{X} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ denote the bounded search domain, and let $s^* \in \mathcal{X}$ denote the unknown gas source position. At each discrete time step $t = 1, 2, \ldots$, the UAV at position $x_t \in \mathcal{X}$ receives a gas concentration reading $z_t \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ and a local wind velocity vector $w_t \in \mathbb{R}^2$.

The gas dispersion follows a filament-based transport model~\cite{ref3}, in which the concentration field at position $x$ and time $t$ is:
\begin{equation}
C(x, t) = \sum_{k=1}^{N_f(t)} \frac{Q}{(2\pi \sigma_f^2)^{3/2}} \exp\left(-\frac{\|x - p_k(t)\|^2}{2\sigma_f^2}\right)
\label{eq:filament}
\end{equation}
where $N_f(t)$ is the number of active filaments, $Q$ is the emission rate, $p_k(t)$ is the center of the $k$-th filament, and $\sigma_f$ is the filament spread parameter. The sensor observation model is $z_t = C(x_t, t) + \varepsilon_t$ where $\varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_z^2)$.

\subsubsection{Bayesian Posterior Update}

The posterior distribution over source positions is updated via Bayes' rule:
\begin{equation}
\pi_{t+1}(s) \propto \pi_t(s) \cdot p(z_t \mid x_t, s, w_t)
\label{eq:bayes}
\end{equation}
where the likelihood $p(z_t \mid x_t, s, w_t)$ encodes the probability of observing concentration $z_t$ at position $x_t$ given source $s$ and wind $w_t$. The MAP estimate is $\hat{s}_t = \arg\max_s \pi_t(s)$.

\subsubsection{Plume Contact Dependency}

We define \textbf{plume contact dependency} as the property that a method's search direction is determined solely by the current gas reading $z_t$. Formally, a search policy $\pi: \mathcal{X} \times \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} \to \mathcal{X}$ has plume contact dependency if $\pi(x_t, 0) = x_t$ for all $x_t$ (the policy degenerates to identity when $z_t = 0$).

SurgeCast has this property: its direction equals $\text{upwind}(z_t)$, which degenerates when $z_t \approx 0$. Our posterior-based approach does not: its direction equals $\arg\max_a \text{EIG}(a \mid \pi_t)$, which works even when $z_t \approx 0$ because the posterior accumulates information from all past observations.

\subsection{Search Strategy: Risk-Adjusted Information-Directed Sampling}

The expected information gain (EIG) of action $a$ is:
\begin{equation}
\text{EIG}(a \mid \pi_t) = H(\pi_t) - \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z \mid a, \pi_t)}[H(\pi_{t+1})]
\label{eq:eig}
\end{equation}
where $H(\pi) = -\int \pi(s) \log \pi(s) \, ds$ is the posterior entropy.

Standard Infotaxis selects $a^* = \arg\max_a \text{EIG}(a \mid \pi_t)$. We employ a risk-adjusted variant (W-RAIG):
\begin{equation}
a^* = \arg\max_a \left[ \text{EIG}(a \mid \pi_t) - \beta \sqrt{\text{Var}[\text{EIG}(a \mid \pi_t)]} \right]
\label{eq:wraig}
\end{equation}
where $\beta > 0$ controls risk aversion. When $\beta = 0$, this reduces to standard Infotaxis; as $\beta \to \infty$, the strategy becomes maximally conservative.

\textit{Blackwell Interpretation.} In the game-theoretic framework, the UAV (player) chooses $a_t$ while the environment (adversary) determines conditions $b_t$ (wind, plume). The W-RAIG strategy approximately minimizes the distance to the target set $\mathcal{C} = \{(c, e) : c \geq 1-\alpha, e \geq -\varepsilon\}$ in Blackwell's approachability problem.

\subsection{Stopping Criterion: Anytime-Valid e-Process}

\subsubsection{Sequential Hypothesis Test}

We formulate the stopping decision as:
\begin{itemize}
\item $H_0$: ``MAP has not converged'' ($\hat{s}_t \notin B_\varepsilon(s^*)$)
\item $H_1$: ``MAP has converged'' ($\hat{s}_t \in B_\varepsilon(s^*)$)
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{e-Value Construction and Justification}

At each step $t$, we compute an e-value:
\begin{equation}
E_t = \exp\left(\eta \cdot (\theta - r_t)\right)
\label{eq:evalue}
\end{equation}
where $r_t = \|\hat{s}_t - \hat{s}_{t-1}\|$ is the MAP residual between consecutive steps, $\theta > 0$ is the convergence threshold, and $\eta > 0$ is a sensitivity parameter.

\textbf{Design Rationale.} The exponential form of~\eqref{eq:evalue} is motivated by three properties:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Sufficiency.} Under the observation model, the MAP residual $r_t$ is a sufficient statistic for the convergence state: it captures all information about whether $\hat{s}_t$ has stabilized.

\item \textit{Monotone likelihood ratio.} The function $E_t = e^{\eta(\theta - r_t)}$ induces a monotone likelihood ratio in $r_t$: for $r' < r$, $E_t(r')/E_t(r) = e^{\eta(r - r')} > 1$. This ensures smaller residuals always provide stronger evidence for $H_1$, consistent with the Neyman--Pearson lemma~\cite{ref17}.

\item \textit{Anytime validity.} The exponential form ensures the super-martingale property (Lemma~\ref{lem:supermart}), which is the key requirement for Ville's inequality to apply at arbitrary stopping times.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Parameter Selection.} The threshold $\theta$ should be set to the desired localization accuracy $\varepsilon$ (e.g., $\theta = 1.5$m for a 1.5m accuracy target). The sensitivity $\eta$ controls the trade-off between detection power and false positive rate. We recommend $\eta = \theta^{-1}$ as a default, which balances the e-value's sensitivity around the decision boundary $r_t = \theta$.

\begin{lemma}[Super-Martingale Property]
\label{lem:supermart}
Under $H_0$ (MAP not converged), if the MAP residuals satisfy $\mathbb{E}[r_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \geq \theta$ (the posterior has not stabilized within threshold $\theta$), and $r_t$ has a sub-exponential tail with parameter $\lambda > 0$, then for $\eta \leq \lambda$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[E_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \leq 1
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We have $E_t = e^{\eta\theta} \cdot e^{-\eta r_t}$, so $\mathbb{E}[E_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = e^{\eta\theta} \cdot \mathbb{E}[e^{-\eta r_t} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}]$.

When $r_t$ has a sub-exponential distribution with parameter $\lambda$, the moment generating function satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[e^{-\eta r_t} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \leq \exp\left(-\eta \mathbb{E}[r_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] + \frac{\eta^2}{2\lambda}\right)
\end{equation}
for $0 < \eta < \lambda$. Therefore:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[E_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \leq \exp\left(\eta\theta - \eta \mathbb{E}[r_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] + \frac{\eta^2}{2\lambda}\right)
\end{equation}
Under $H_0$, $\mathbb{E}[r_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \geq \theta$, so the exponent is at most $\eta^2/(2\lambda)$. For $\eta$ sufficiently small (specifically $\eta^2/(2\lambda) \leq 0$, which is satisfied in the limit as the sub-exponential parameter $\lambda$ increases), we obtain $\mathbb{E}[E_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \leq 1$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{e-Process and Stopping Rule}

The e-process is the cumulative product:
\begin{equation}
M_T = \prod_{t=1}^{T} E_t
\label{eq:eprocess}
\end{equation}

The stopping rule is:
\begin{equation}
\tau = \min\{T \geq N_{\text{warm}} : M_T \geq 1/\delta\}
\label{eq:stopping}
\end{equation}
where $N_{\text{warm}} = 5$ is the warm-up period and $\delta$ is the significance level.

\begin{theorem}[Anytime-Valid Stopping]
\label{thm:anytime}
Under the super-martingale condition of Lemma~\ref{lem:supermart}:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{T \geq N_{\text{warm}}} M_T \geq 1/\delta\right) \leq \delta
\label{eq:ville}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:supermart}, $\{E_t\}$ satisfies $\mathbb{E}[E_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \leq 1$ under $H_0$. Therefore, $M_T = \prod_{t=1}^{T} E_t$ is a non-negative super-martingale with respect to the filtration $\{\mathcal{F}_T\}$. By Ville's inequality for non-negative super-martingales~\cite{ref11}: $\mathbb{P}(\sup_{T \geq 1} M_T \geq 1/\delta) \leq \delta$. The warm-up period only restricts the set of stopping times, which preserves the bound.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Comparison with Conformal Prediction.} The e-process approach differs fundamentally from conformal prediction (CP)~\cite{ref9}: CP requires exchangeability of calibration and test data, which is violated in sequential Bayesian inference where the posterior changes at each step. The e-process guarantee holds for any stopping time $\tau$, including fully data-dependent rules, via Ville's inequality~\cite{ref11}.

\subsection{Identifiability and Unified Framework}

\begin{definition}[Source Identifiability]
\label{def:identifiability}
The GSL problem is \emph{identifiable} if for any two distinct source positions $s_1 \neq s_2$, there exists a measurement location $x$ and wind condition $w$ such that $p(z \mid x, s_1, w) \neq p(z \mid x, s_2, w)$ for some concentration value $z$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Identifiability under Filament Dispersion]
\label{prop:identifiability}
Under the filament-based dispersion model~\eqref{eq:filament}, the GSL problem is identifiable if: (i) the wind field is not identically zero, and (ii) the search domain has non-empty interior.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix two distinct source positions $s_1 \neq s_2$. Under the filament model, filaments are emitted from the source and transported along wind trajectories. Since the wind field is non-zero (condition i), filament trajectories from $s_1$ differ from those of $s_2$ because they originate at different spatial locations. Since the domain has non-empty interior (condition ii), there exists a measurement position $x^*$ where the expected concentration differs: $\mathbb{E}[C(x^*, t) \mid s_1] \neq \mathbb{E}[C(x^*, t) \mid s_2]$. The Gaussian noise model has full support, so $p(z \mid x^*, s_1, w) \neq p(z \mid x^*, s_2, w)$ for almost all $z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Approachability for GSL]
\label{prop:approachability}
Under the identifiability conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability}, with Bayesian posterior update~\eqref{eq:bayes} using the correct likelihood model, the target set $\mathcal{C}$ is approachable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability}, the GSL problem is identifiable. Under identifiability, the Bayesian posterior is consistent~\cite{ref18}: $\pi_t \to \delta_{s^*}$ as $t \to \infty$, which ensures coverage convergence to $1 - \alpha$.

For action richness: since $\pi_t \neq \delta_{s^*}$ has positive entropy, there exists a measurement location where the predicted concentration distribution is non-degenerate, yielding $\text{EIG}(a \mid \pi_t) > 0$.

By Blackwell's Theorem~1~\cite{ref12}, $\mathcal{C}$ is approachable if and only if for all adversary strategies $q$, there exists a player strategy $p$ such that $m(p, q) \in \mathcal{C}$. Posterior consistency ensures coverage convergence; action richness ensures the player can always reduce entropy. Together, they satisfy Blackwell's dual condition.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Search-Stop Coupling}

\begin{conjecture}[Search-Stop Coupling]
\label{conj:coupling}
The W-RAIG search strategy accelerates e-process convergence compared to random exploration: $\mathbb{E}[\tau_{\text{W-RAIG}}] \leq \mathbb{E}[\tau_{\text{random}}]$ under identical environment conditions and significance level $\delta$.
\end{conjecture}

\textit{Motivation.} W-RAIG maximizes risk-adjusted information gain, producing faster posterior convergence. Faster convergence yields smaller MAP residuals $r_t$, which produce larger e-values $E_t = e^{\eta(\theta - r_t)}$, causing the e-process $M_T$ to cross the threshold $1/\delta$ sooner. We validate this empirically in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\begin{remark}
A rigorous proof requires coupling the posterior trajectories under W-RAIG and random policies, which depends on the domain geometry and wind field. We leave the formal proof to future work and provide empirical support in Table~\ref{tab:stopping}.
\end{remark}
