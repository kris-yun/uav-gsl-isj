## Existing-data screening already completed

### A. Source vs transport factorial audit
Using 12 controlled histories:
H01/H02/H03 × {SA,SB} × {fast,slow}.

Observed:
- source/transport separability is horizon dependent;
- early H02/H03 can have no source effect or source≈transport interaction;
- coarse binary HIT representation can remove source signal.

### B. Predictable-vs-innovation proxy
Causal smoothing / source-blind predictive proxies:
- H02/H03: predictable component often more source-dominant than innovation after support appears;
- H01 provides a critical counterexample: generic predictive compression can erase late rare source-defining evidence.

### C. Extreme-event/intermittency proxy
Fixed source-blind tail observables:
- q95/q99, max/top-tail mass, exceedance fractions, first-arrival.
- H01 240 s: tail branch rescued held-wind source identity lost by a generic predictive branch.
- H02 180 s: tail branch also recovered source identity.
- H03: tail branch complementary but not uniformly superior.

Conclusion:
- rare/intermittent events are not nuisance by default; they can be the source-defining evidence.
- M2 is mechanistically justified as a complement to M1.

### D. Direct nuisance-removal / alignment negative control
Train-window cross-wind nuisance projection and naive explicit-wind conditioning:
- H01 and H03 degraded under projection;
- H02 largely neutral;
- wind conditioning did not consistently improve held-wind separation.

Conclusion:
- reject simple source-invariance / nuisance-projection auxiliary.
- candidate-dependent transport effects remain explicit.
