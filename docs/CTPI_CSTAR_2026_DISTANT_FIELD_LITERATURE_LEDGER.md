# CSTAR verified 2026 distant-field literature ledger

Date checked: 2026-09-06
Purpose: document what scientific object is transferred into CSTAR, and what is **not** claimed.

All items below were checked against publisher/proceedings pages. CSTAR does not claim that these papers solve robotic GSL; they are distant-field mother principles used for a new domain-specific construction.

## M1 — functional genomics / single-cell perturbation biology

### 1. Dimitrov et al. — causal/mechanistic perturbation modelling

Dimitrov, D., Schrod, S., Rohbeck, M. et al. **Interpretation, extrapolation and perturbation of single cells.** Nature Reviews Genetics 27, 349–370 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s41576-025-00920-4`.

Verified publisher finding: single-cell analysis is moving from descriptive atlases toward causal effects and mechanistic relationships; the review connects representation learning, causal inference, mechanistic discovery, disentanglement and perturbation.

Transferred principle: do not infer a stable cause from observational correlation alone; use intervention variation and explicit disentanglement.

CSTAR adaptation: source location is the persistent target cause; wind/transport, release strength and sensor memory are nuisance mechanisms that can be deliberately varied in offline intervention pairs.

### 2. Shen et al. — autonomous versus microenvironment effects

Shen, K., Seow, W.Y., Keng, C.T. et al. **Spatial perturb-seq: single-cell functional genomics within intact tissue architecture.** Nature Communications 17, 3018 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s41467-026-69677-6`.

Verified publisher finding: targeted perturbations are read together with spatial context, allowing cell-autonomous and non-cell-autonomous microenvironmental effects to be separated.

Transferred principle: the response attributed to a target must be distinguished from contextual effects generated by its surrounding environment.

CSTAR adaptation: source identity must be separated from transport/microenvironmental context instead of treating the full observed gas pattern as source identity.

### 3. Nourreddine et al. — perturbation phenotype atlas

Nourreddine, S., Doctor, Y., Dailamy, A. et al. **A genome-scale CRISPRi perturbation atlas of human induced pluripotent stem cells.** Nature Biotechnology (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s41587-026-03199-w`.

Verified publisher finding: 11,692 gene perturbations across >2.5 million cells are mapped to transcriptional phenotypes and functional relationships.

Transferred principle: systematic controlled perturbations can expose stable causal identity/functional structure that is not reliably recoverable from unperturbed correlations.

CSTAR adaptation: construct same-source/different-nuisance and different-source/matched-nuisance intervention tables as first-class training/evaluation objects.

### 4. Guo et al. — dual-branch perturbation dynamics

Guo, Y., Zhang, H., Hu, H. et al. **Modelling drug-induced cellular perturbation responses with a biologically informed dual-branch transformer.** Nature Machine Intelligence 8, 96–112 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s42256-025-01165-w`.

Verified publisher finding: XPert separately encodes pre- and post-perturbation cellular states, disentangles intrinsic transcriptional patterns from perturbation-induced regulatory shifts, and models dose–time dynamics.

Transferred principle: baseline/intrinsic state and intervention-induced change should be represented by distinct pathways rather than collapsed into one latent.

CSTAR adaptation: separate gas-response and transport/context branches, plus explicit source-stable and nuisance/amplitude outputs.

### 5. Driessen et al. — conditional response maps across unseen contexts

Driessen, A., Rajwade, D.A., Harsanyi, B. et al. **Conditional Monge Gap enables generalizable single-cell perturbation modelling.** Nature Machine Intelligence 8, 984–996 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s42256-026-01242-8`.

Verified publisher finding: one conditional optimal-transport estimator learns perturbation responses across drug/dose/cell contexts and improves extrapolation to unseen conditions.

Transferred principle: learn a common context-conditioned response family rather than a separate map per condition.

CSTAR adaptation: one candidate scorer/encoder must operate across Houses, wind realizations and source strengths; no House-specific classifier or calibration table.

## M1 — causal-representation theory support

### 6. Kim et al. — internal auxiliaries

Kim, K., Nam, H., Hwang, I., Lee, S. **On Causal Representation Learning with Internal Auxiliaries.** UAI 2026, PMLR 337:3061–3082.

Verified proceedings finding: observable sources inside a mixing process can serve as internal conditioning variables; under stated assumptions, latent source subspaces become identifiable up to an ISA-style equivalence.

CSTAR use: wind, pose, geometry and sensor state are treated as internal causal auxiliaries explaining the mixing of source into observed gas, not merely concatenated nuisance features.

### 7. Baumgartner et al. — local causal structure in dynamical systems

Baumgartner, M.W., Lei, A., Watson, J., Posner, I. **Disentangling Dynamical Systems: Causal Representation Learning Meets Local Sparse Attention.** CLeaR 2026, PMLR 323:119–165.

Verified proceedings finding: a graphical identifiability criterion is given for disentangling system parameters from trajectories, and local state-dependent causal structure can be necessary beyond a global graph; implementation uses sparsity-regularized attention.

CSTAR use: M1 must respect temporal causality and test local/physical support instead of unrestricted history mixing. We do not import its theorem as a proof for GSL; we use it as the theoretical motivation for a GSL-specific identifiability/falsification programme.

### 8. Li et al. — preserving causal structure through representation

Li, Y. et al. **CARL: Preserving Causal Structure in Representation Learning.** ICLR 2026.

Verified conference finding: nonlinear encoders can introduce spurious dependencies or remove mediators; CARL explicitly constrains conditional-independence and Markov-boundary preservation.

CSTAR use: source representation must be audited for nuisance leakage and causal-structure destruction; task accuracy alone is not enough to label M1 causal.

## M2 — chemical physics / rare-event transition theory

### 9. Breebaart et al. — committor as rare-transition coordinate

Breebaart, R.S., Lazzeri, G., Covino, R., Bolhuis, P.G. **Understanding Mechanisms of Molecular Rare Events from Start to Finish.** Physical Review Letters 136, 168001 (2026). DOI: `10.1103/lk32-njx7`.

Verified APS finding: the committor is treated as the ideal reaction coordinate for rare transitions; the work learns it iteratively from reweighted transition-path ensembles.

Transferred principle: for intermittent phenomena, predict the probability of reaching an event and its transition path, not merely a mean field.

CSTAR adaptation: define gas encounter as a first-passage event along `do(route)` and predict its horizon-dependent committor.

### 10. Contreras Arredondo et al. — learned committor from high-dimensional configuration

Contreras Arredondo, S., Tang, C., Talmazan, R.A. et al. **Learning the committor without collective variables.** Nature Computational Science 6, 350–357 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s43588-026-00958-2`.

Verified publisher finding: a graph neural network predicts the committor directly from atomic coordinates without hand-crafted collective variables and recovers physically meaningful transition information.

Transferred principle: the event probability can be learned directly from structured high-dimensional state rather than forced through a single hand-designed scalar coordinate.

CSTAR adaptation: CPO consumes geometry, sparse wind history, sensor state, source hypothesis and route; it need not compress all transport into one plume parameter.

### 11. Chang & Sapsis — rare/extreme regime awareness

Chang, K., Sapsis, T.P. **Extreme Event Aware (η-) Learning.** Nature Communications (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s41467-026-76811-x`.

Verified publisher finding: statistical constraints on an observable indicative of extremeness can reduce uncertainty in rare/unseen extreme regimes even when extreme events are scarce in training data.

CSTAR use: encounter/no-encounter tails must be scored and calibrated separately; average RMSE is insufficient for M2 qualification.

### 12. Durumeric et al. — stochastic coarse-grained dynamics

Durumeric, A.E.P., Chen, Y., Pasos-Trejo, A.S. et al. **Learning data-efficient coarse-grained molecular dynamics from forces and noise.** Nature Communications 17, 2493 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s41467-026-70818-0`.

Transferred principle: reduced dynamics should model both structured drift and stochastic/noise contributions instead of collapsing unresolved dynamics to a deterministic mean.

CSTAR use: CPO outputs a probability law over future encounter time and concentration marks, not just a deterministic predicted concentration.

## M2 — scientific-computing support

### 13. Long et al. — operator learning across changing geometry

Long, Z., Zhou, Q., Zhu, A. et al. **Deep neural operator for free boundary problems.** Nature Machine Intelligence 8, 806–817 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s42256-026-01233-9`.

Verified publisher finding: a neural-operator framework uses topological conjugacy to handle solution operators on evolving/unknown domains.

CSTAR use: geometry is an explicit conditioning object so M2 is not a fixed House grid predictor. We do not claim the GSL domain is a free-boundary problem.

### 14. Wang et al. — learn missing physics around a trusted executable prior

Wang, H., Wang, Q., Yuan, C., Wu, K. **Learning missing physics from legacy simulators with alternating neural integrators.** Nature Communications 17, 7877 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s41467-026-74002-2`.

Verified publisher finding: ANI keeps a reusable executable prior inside an alternating composition and learns structured discrepancy to higher-fidelity dynamics.

CSTAR use: retain the audited V2 conservative/FOPDT physics as a prior or feature generator and learn only the missing encounter-law correction. We do not claim ANI itself is implemented verbatim.

## M3 — systems/cognitive neuroscience

### 15. Tang et al. — goal-directed prospective theta sweeps

Tang, W., Mei, X., Harvey, R.E. et al. **Goal-directed hippocampal theta sweeps during memory-guided navigation.** Nature Neuroscience 29, 2214–2224 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s41593-026-02364-3`.

Verified publisher finding: learning-dependent theta sequences sweep ahead and predict upcoming goal-directed trajectories; they coordinate with prefrontal activity and replay.

Transferred principle: evaluate prospective trajectories rather than only the immediate next observation.

CSTAR adaptation: PHS scores complete equal-horizon physically feasible future route sweeps.

### 16. Yu et al. — sweeps point to remembered goals independent of movement direction

Yu, C., Ji, Z., Ormond, J. et al. **Hippocampal theta sweeps indicate goal direction during navigation.** Nature Neuroscience 29, 2225–2236 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s41593-026-02365-2`.

Verified publisher finding: theta sweeps form vectors toward remembered goal locations independently of current heading/movement, and stronger goal modulation precedes correct navigation choices.

Transferred principle: internal future-state evaluation can be separated from the robot’s instantaneous motion direction.

CSTAR adaptation: route scoring uses predicted future observation laws, not only the local wind direction or current concentration gradient.

### 17. He et al. — replay constructs candidate sequences

He, L., Wang, X., Zhang, J. et al. **Human hippocampal ripples coordinate planning sequences and compositional representations in neocortex.** Nature Neuroscience 29, 1711–1721 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s41593-026-02291-3`.

Verified publisher finding: hippocampal replay reorganizes building blocks into candidate sequences and predicts efficient inferential behavior.

CSTAR use: candidate future routes are explicit sequence objects evaluated before execution, not merely endpoint scalar values.

### 18. D’Ambrogio et al. — information symmetry across alternatives

D’Ambrogio, S., Grohn, J., Khalighinejad, N. et al. **Interpretable abstractions of artificial neural networks predict behavior and neural activity during human information gathering.** Nature Neuroscience 29, 2036–2047 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s41593-026-02342-9`.

Verified publisher finding: the recovered information-sampling computation depends primarily on **relative evidence across alternatives**, revealing an information-symmetry principle that outperforms standard option-wise uncertainty accounts and generalizes to an independent dataset.

Transferred principle: information is valuable when it resolves competing alternatives relative to each other, not merely where absolute uncertainty is high.

CSTAR adaptation: PHS minimizes posterior-weighted pairwise Bhattacharyya confusion among source hypotheses. This exact score is ours; it is not claimed to be the neuroscience paper’s equation.

### 19. Frank et al. — predictive uncertainty prepares encoding

Frank, D., Moratti, S., Hellerstedt, R. et al. **Human hippocampal ripples tune cortical responses based on predicted uncertainty.** Nature Neuroscience 29, 1987–1998 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s41593-026-02345-6`.

Transferred principle: predicted uncertainty can govern where new information should be encoded/used to update internal representations.

CSTAR use: M3 acts only through an explicit predicted observation distribution supplied by M2; it does not assign information value from geometric distance or posterior mass alone.

## Robotic-GSL collision check / claims we must avoid

### 20. Zhang et al. — 2026 robotic-olfaction review

Zhang, W., Tang, W., Wan, Z. et al. **Advanced electronic noses for future robotic olfaction.** npj Robotics 4, 11 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s44182-025-00071-y`.

The review surveys gradient, anemotaxis and probabilistic GSL and emphasizes turbulence/wind challenges. It is a field map for collision checks, not a mother method.

### 21. Shigaki et al. — already-existing insect-inspired robotic strategy

Shigaki, S., Yokota, K., Sekiwa, R. et al. **Insect-inspired adaptive behavioral compensation strategy against olfactory sensory deficiency for robotic odor source localization.** npj Robotics 4, 12 (2026). DOI: `10.1038/s44182-026-00080-5`.

Claim boundary: CSTAR must not call PHS novel merely because it is biologically/neuroscience inspired.

### 22. Xu et al. — already-existing bumblebee spatial memory navigation

Xu, T., Guo, Y., Wu, Z., Wu, J. **A Bumblebee-Inspired Spatial Memory Navigation Framework for Robotic Odor Source Localization.** Biomimetics 11(5), 350 (2026). DOI: `10.3390/biomimetics11050350`.

Claim boundary: PHS novelty, if eventually supported, is prospective **source-hypothesis-distribution separation** using an action-conditioned future observation law, not generic memory or bio-inspired search.

## Current novelty position

The literature above supports the scientific ingredients, not a priority claim. Before manuscript submission perform a broader GSL collision search for:

- causal representation / invariant source representations under transport intervention;
- committor / first-passage predictive observation operators for GSL;
- route-level source-hypothesis confusion minimization;
- combinations of source causal representation + action-conditioned stochastic transport law + prospective active resolution.

Until that search is complete, use `candidate novelty` rather than `first` or `first-ever`.
